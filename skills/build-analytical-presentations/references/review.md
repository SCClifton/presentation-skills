# Review and acceptance

Use the checks relevant to the requested deliverable. A storyboard needs argument and evidence review. A presentation file also needs visual and file checks.

## Argument

- Can the audience tell what they are being helped to understand or decide?
- Does each section supply a premise, explanation, evidence, alternative, or resolution that the argument needs?
- Do the headlines communicate a coherent progression?
- Are important concepts defined before they are used?
- Does the conclusion follow from the evidence actually shown?
- Does the ending resolve the opening promise and identify any remaining decision or uncertainty?

## Writing

- Are claims precise, direct, and bounded?
- Are bullets parallel, and paragraphs connected where causal explanation matters?
- Have slogans, decorative jargon, duplicate context, and unsupported superlatives been removed?
- Does selective emphasis identify the key relationship rather than colour most of the text?
- Are technical terms necessary and understandable to this audience?

## Evidence

- Can the reader find the evidence behind consequential claims?
- Are units, dates, denominators, baseline, population, and status clear?
- Do charts and prose agree?
- Are measurements, source statements, calculations, estimates, proposals, and targets distinguishable where confusion is possible?
- Are uncertainty and causal limits stated close to the claims they qualify?
- Are unverified placeholders confined to an explicitly labelled draft?

## Layout and file

- Is the reading order clear on every slide?
- Are comparisons aligned and genuinely comparable?
- Are chart labels, tables, sources, and diagram relationships legible at the intended size?
- Are spacing, margins, navigation, and object styles coherent?
- Have clipping, overflow, collisions, unintended blank slides, stale page references, and broken links been checked?
- Are editable objects present where the delivery requires them?
- Has the final saved file been inspected, rather than only its authoring preview?

## Adaptation

- Does the depth suit the audience's prior knowledge and delivery setting?
- Has the current organisation's template been respected?
- Is information from other organisations absent unless the user expressly supplied it for this task?
- Can the same method be used with another approved template without copying unrelated content?

## Prioritise repairs

Repair an unsupported conclusion, misleading scale, missing decision, or unreadable evidence before cosmetic inconsistencies. Preserve correct work. Do not rebuild an entire deck to fix a local error.

For a new deck, compare the result with the brief. For a revision, also compare with the source file to confirm that user edits and necessary content survived.

## Handoff

Deliver the requested file or storyboard with a concise statement of what it contains, the validation performed, and material limitations. Name a blocked export or missing evidence plainly. Do not claim an organisation has accepted, approved, tested, or deployed something merely because the presentation describes a proposal to do so.
